# Skooby: first case-file launch experiment

## One promise

**Paste a public wallet address. Leave with a readable case file.**

Audience hypothesis: people learning to read Bitcoin/Ethereum activity and creators explaining public blockchain transactions. Begin with education and inspectable receipts. Avoid claims about identifying people, proving scams, predicting prices, or complete wallet histories.

## Sharing path

Visitor opens a fictional demo → investigates an address → downloads a branded card or explicitly creates an expiring report → recipient sees the evidence and opens their own case.

The free result is the demonstration. Keep login behind private casebook saving. Retain visible provenance and sample limits on every export.

## Seven-day experiment

1. **Before release:** verify live provider responses and configured Redis; run all automated checks; inspect mobile layout and downloaded/social images; prepare a 20–30 second screen recording of the synthetic demo.
2. **Days 1–2:** publish one honest demo post on an owner-selected channel. Ask five willing testers to open a case and explain what the graph means. Collect confusion and failure reports manually; never collect seed phrases or private keys.
3. **Days 3–4:** fix the largest observed first-use problem. Show a second example explaining why two inputs do not prove common ownership. Keep the CTA identical so the result is understandable.
4. **Days 5–7:** review daily aggregate counts and tester feedback. Expand distribution only if users can explain the result accurately and several choose to share without prompting. Otherwise improve the report before adding more chains or paid plans.

Decision targets are hypotheses, not forecasts: five completed tester tasks, zero misleading ownership interpretations in those five sessions, and at least three voluntary shares. This small sample is directional; it does not estimate a population conversion rate.

Daily counters show activity volume. Manual consent-based tester feedback supplies understanding and willingness-to-share evidence. This release does not measure unique visitors, seven-day retention, referral coefficients, or ranking placement.

## Ready-to-edit launch copy

> A blockchain explorer gives you a transaction list. Skooby turns a public Bitcoin or Ethereum address into a readable case file: a transaction map, source receipts, and a card you can share. No wallet connection. Try the fictional demo: https://skooby.app/tracker?demo=1

## Demo recording outline

- 0–4 seconds: “What does this wallet trail actually tell us?” Show the fictional label.
- 4–12 seconds: select a transaction and its inputs/outputs; explain that connections do not prove identity.
- 12–20 seconds: reveal the factual summary and receipts, then download the case card.
- 20–25 seconds: show the exported card and the public-address CTA.

Use an openly documented example or the synthetic demo. Do not imply a real person's address belongs to them without reliable evidence. Publish only after the deployed result is verified. These drafts have not been posted or sent anywhere.

## Next expansion, if the experiment supports it

Prioritize a clearer first report, reliable share delivery, and provider capacity. Then assess contract/internal transfer support and better history pagination. Automatic watchlist notifications need their own scheduler, deduplication, delivery preferences, and delivery-status UI before being marketed as active alerts.
